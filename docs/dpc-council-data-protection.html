Running the poll and collecting returns

Once the station is set up, staff can continue issuing ballots through a connection outage. The tablet uploads saved records when the connection returns.

If a tablet is lost or stops working

  1. Contact your elections duty lead. The Presiding Officer reports the issue and secures the ballot stock and station paperwork. A T2K engineer is on call on election day by live chat or phone.
  2. Check what has arrived before moving to a spare. Your council reconciles the records DPC has received against the available paperwork and physical stock. DPC can transfer station access to an approved spare once reconciliation is complete.
  3. Use your council’s paper contingency if needed. Your council decides when to switch, supplies the backup register and forms, and reconciles paper and digital records.

Records saved only on a missing or unusable tablet cannot be recovered remotely. A spare can retrieve records that have already reached DPC.

Check receipt

In Data exports, Closed and reported means the signed close reached DPC and the returns are ready. Outstanding can include a station that has signed off offline.

Export and confirm

Once the signed close is accepted, council administrators securely export the records in the dashboard. The council confirms it has its copies and T2K confirms the completed export in writing before deletion.

Data, access and offline protection

Fasthosts hosts the live DPC service and backups in the UK on behalf of T2K Group Limited. The dashboard uses role-based access and approved tablets receive encrypted station packages.

Council systems

The council holds the source register and prepares extracts for import. It controls access to its own systems and exported copies. The register can include names, addresses, postcodes, elector numbers, polling districts, eligibility markers, dates of birth if supplied, and proxy details. Anonymous entries omit names, addresses, postcodes and dates of birth.

DPC hosted service

The service holds imported registers, station and contest settings, register snapshots, encrypted station packages, polling records and signed returns. Ballot issue records link elector references to paper serials. Station close records include the Presiding Officer’s name and drawn signature. DPC receives device status and, where enabled, the tablet’s last reported location.

Council viewers can read register data for their organisation. Editors and administrators can manage it within their permissions. Organisation administrators and DPC superadmins can access identifying voter ID reports and signed returns. DPC superadmins have cross-organisation access.

Approved Android tablets

Tablets cache encrypted packages for their council. The Presiding Officer uses the station QR code and matching five-word password to unlock its register, contests and ballot paper ranges. Authorised station staff use the activated tablet.

Android Keystore protects the activated station data, which is stored in private app storage excluded from device backups. An encrypted local journal stores polling records pending upload. Signed device requests identify the approved tablet, and the service recognises retried events to avoid duplicate records.

Voter ID records

The journal and hosted service retain the accepted document category and relevant outcomes, including turn-aways, refusal reasons and private checks. DPC keeps no images or document numbers.

Device recovery and proof of receipt

A connection outage, a device transfer and a device wipe have different effects on the local polling journal.

What a replacement can recover

A station transfer requires confirmation that the original tablet is out of service and that all issued papers and corresponding numbers list records have been reconciled. The approved replacement connects to DPC and retrieves accepted station issue records before it can issue ballots offline.

Issues saved only on the original tablet cannot be transferred. If that tablet becomes usable again, it can retry pending uploads while its encrypted journal remains intact and it still has station access.

Revoke station access

Authorised council staff can revoke access in the dashboard. The server blocks station access immediately. An offline tablet acts on the change when it reconnects. Station revocation preserves the encrypted journal on the tablet.

Disable and wipe a device

The on-call T2K engineer can disable the device and trigger a wipe of DPC app data on reconnection. This removes unsynced records. The council and T2K assess the recovery options before deciding on a wipe.

How DPC verifies receipt

  • During the poll. Dashboard totals use accepted ballot issues. Device details show the last heartbeat and the reported pending issue count. A stale heartbeat or unknown count leaves the current tablet status unverified.
  • At close. The server validates the submitted journal against its accepted records and verifies the ballot paper accounts before making the signed returns available. Data exports shows last data received for outstanding stations and the accounts received.
  • On the tablet. Data is pending appears while upload is incomplete. The tablet confirms once the signed close and all local station records have been acknowledged.

Retention, supplier assurance and the DPA

The council acts as controller and T2K Group Limited acts as processor on the council’s documented instructions. Agree a written UK GDPR Article 28 data processing agreement (DPA) before processing live elector data.

Post-election return and deletion

  1. Export. After signed close, council administrators securely download the marked register and corresponding numbers list as PDF or CSV, the signed ballot paper account as PDF, and voter ID notes, refusals and totals as CSV.
  2. Confirm. The council confirms it has all copies and applies its own retention schedule. T2K confirms in writing that the export is complete before deletion begins.
  3. Delete. T2K wipes the encrypted DPC app data remotely and deletes live election data from the hosted service. An offline tablet receives the wipe on reconnection. Audit logs and backups are kept for 90 days after live deletion unless earlier deletion is requested, subject to legally required retention.

Incident escalation

T2K informs the council’s designated contact without undue delay once aware of a personal data breach. The council assesses whether to notify the ICO or affected people. Agree escalation contacts and wipe authority before polling day.
